# Focus group: discussion guide

A script for the focus group interview after the third workshop: who to invite, what to ask, what to have them do, and what we want to learn. The answers themselves (notes, recordings) stay outside this repository, like the rest of the user research (see [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#current-state)).

**Status: a first draft, 2026-10-21.**

## Who we design for: hobbyist to hobbyist

The service is made **by moviegoing hobbyists for moviegoing hobbyists**: people who go to the cinema often, follow retrospectives and series, care about versions and formats, and travel for the right screening. We don't design for the average moviegoer. If the hobbyist is served well, the occasional moviegoer is too: one place with every cinema in it works for everyone.

So the focus group is made of hobbyists. Their needs are the sharpest, and their habits show what the service should become.

## Focus group, not a usability test

|               | Focus group (this)                                             | Usability test                         |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Who           | 5–8 people at once, with a moderator                           | One person at a time                   |
| What we learn | Habits, needs, priorities, the words people use, disagreements | Where people get stuck, what they miss |
| How           | Discussion; people react to each other                         | Tasks while thinking aloud, observed   |
| Watch out for | Groupthink, loud voices, talk of what people _would_ do        | Few opinions, no group dynamics        |

We also want to see the website in use, so the session has a short **individual** hands-on part: each person on a laptop of their own, writing down what confused them, before the group discusses it. That keeps the group from echoing its loudest member.

## Participants

- **5–8 hobbyists**: people who go to the cinema at least twice a month, or who follow film clubs, festivals, retrospectives or a cinema's own series.
- **A mix within the hobby:** an art-house regular, someone who travels for screenings, a festival goer, a film student or film club member, and a big-screen fan (IMAX, 70 mm).
- **Where to find them:** local film clubs (_elokuvakerhot_), festival volunteers, film students, the regulars of small and art-house cinemas, our own circles. Elsis is a candidate.
- **Screening question:** "How many times did you go to the cinema in the last three months, and how did you find out what was on?"

## Before the session

- **Roles:** a moderator (leads, stays neutral, never defends the product) and a note-taker (writes quotes word for word, with a participant code, not a name). A second note-taker if possible.
- **Consent:** tell the participants what we record and why, where the notes are kept and that quotes are anonymised; ask before recording.
- **Material:**
  - a laptop per participant (their own, or one we bring), with the website open (`/fi/haku`, `/fi/elokuvat/…`): more room for the search page's filters than a phone;
  - a laptop with the chat (the MCP connector) on a shared screen;
  - the journey map slides ([journey-a-slide.png](journey-a-slide.png), [journey-b-slide.png](journey-b-slide.png)), printed or on screen;
  - paper and pens for individual notes; sticky notes for the concept round.
- **Check the data that morning:** pick the films and screenings for the tasks from that week's programme (see "Tasks").

## The session (about 90 minutes)

### 1. Welcome (5 min)

- Who we are and what we are making, in one sentence: "a service that gathers the showtimes of every cinema in Finland".
- "We want to hear about _your_ moviegoing. There are no wrong answers, and you can't hurt our feelings: criticism is the most useful thing you can give us."
- Consent and recording.

### 2. Warm-up (10 min)

Round the table:

- "Your name, and the last film you saw in a cinema. Where, and how did you find that screening?"
- "What's the best cinema experience you've had? What made it?"

### 3. Moviegoing as a hobby (20 min)

Open questions; let the conversation run, follow up on what's interesting.

- "How do you keep track of what's on? Which sites, newsletters, social media, apps?"
- "How do you find out about retrospectives, series, film prints, guest screenings?"
- "Have you missed a screening you would have wanted to see? How did that happen?"
- "How far have you travelled for a screening? What makes it worth it?"
- "What matters when you choose a screening: the version, subtitles, the cinema, the hall, the print or format, the time?"
- "What annoys you about the cinemas' own sites?"

Then show the journey maps (A and B):

- "Is this how it goes for you? What's wrong or missing here?"

**Listen for:** whether the pain points of the journey maps come up unprompted; the words people use for things (_näytös_, _sarja_, _kopio_, _klassikko_ …); habits we haven't thought of.

### 4. Hands-on, alone (20 min)

Everyone uses the website on a laptop, alone, for the tasks below, writing down anything that confused them or that they missed. Anyone who'd rather use their phone may: it's how journey A really happens, and the site is built mobile first. Note who used which device, so the findings can be read per device. The moderator and note-takers watch and note where people hesitate, but don't help.

Tasks (pick the details from that week's programme):

1. **The evening** _(should work)_: "You're free tonight in [city]. Find something you'd like to see after 18:00."
2. **The film** _(should work)_: "You want to see [a classic or art-house film playing this week]. Where and when could you see it?"
3. **The double feature** _(should work)_: "Plan a Saturday in Helsinki with two films back to back."
4. **On film** _(a probe: not built)_: "Find a screening on 35 mm this week." (There are prints at Kino Regina and Cinema Niagara, and 70 mm at Bio Rex Lasipalatsi, but the website doesn't show projection yet.)
5. **The series** _(a probe: not built)_: "Find every screening of [a cinema's series, e.g. Kino Regina's director series]."

The probes are expected to fail. We want to see _how_ hobbyists look for these things, and what they'd expect to find.

### 5. Discussing the website (15 min)

- "What was easy? What was hard or confusing?" (everyone reads one note first, then discussion)
- "What did you look for and not find?"
- "On the film and series tasks, what did you try? Where would you have expected it?"
- "Would you use this instead of what you use now? What would it take?"

### 6. The chat (10 min)

A demo on the shared screen: the participants dictate the questions, the moderator types them in. Examples to get going:

- "What classics are on in Helsinki this weekend?"
- "Where can I see [film] on the big screen within 100 km of Jyväskylä?"
- "Anything on film, not digital, this week?"

Then:

- "Would you rather ask like this, or search yourself? When which?"
- "Did you trust the answers? What would make you trust them?"

### 7. Hobbyist features (10 min)

Each concept in a sentence, then reactions. If time is short, start with the first two.

1. **Critics' scores:** "Should a service like this show critics' scores (Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, IMDb), or would you rather not see them before a film?" _This divides people; let it._
2. **Following:** "Would you want to follow a director, a series or a kind of screening, and be told when the next one comes near?"
3. **Personal settings:** "Never dubbed, only original versions, film prints, your home cinemas: which would you set once?"
4. **Your own lists:** "Do you keep a watchlist somewhere (Letterboxd, notes)? Would you want it connected to what's showing?"
5. **Buying:** "Our links take you straight to the cinema's ticket page for that screening. Is that fine, or should buying happen here?"

### 8. Wrap-up (5 min)

- "If you could change one thing about how you find screenings, what would it be?"
- "Anything we didn't ask that we should have?"
- Thanks; tell them how we'll use what they said.

## What we want to learn

These map to the hypotheses in the [journey maps](journey-map.md#hypotheses-to-test):

| Question                                                        | Session part | Hypothesis |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ---------- |
| How do hobbyists find screenings today, and what hurts?         | 3            | 1, 2       |
| Do they start from time and place, or from the film?            | 3, 4         | 3          |
| Is leaving our site to buy from the cinema fine?                | 7            | 5          |
| Do they want critics' scores, or avoid them?                    | 7            | 7          |
| Which personal settings matter; do prints and formats matter?   | 4, 7         | 8          |
| Do they follow directors, series, kinds of screenings?          | 3, 4, 7      | 9          |
| Does the website work for their tasks; where do they get stuck? | 4, 5         | –          |
| Would they use a chat, and would they trust it?                 | 6            | –          |

## Afterwards

- **The same day:** the moderator and note-takers write down their impressions while they're fresh, before talking to each other.
- **Within a week:** collect the notes and quotes onto sticky notes (or a board) and group them by theme (affinity mapping). For each hypothesis: supported, contradicted or unclear, with the quotes behind it.
- **Update the journey maps:** mark what the group confirmed **[V]** (with the date and "focus group"), and correct what it didn't.
- **Tell the team** the three most surprising findings and the three things to build first.

## Moderating tips

- Ask open questions: "How did you find it last time?", not "Wasn't it annoying that …?".
- Ask for stories, not opinions: "Tell me about the last time …" gets closer to real behaviour than "Would you …?".
- Silence is fine: wait a few seconds before helping.
- Draw in the quiet ones by name: "[Name], how is it for you?"; slow down the loud ones politely: "Let's hear from others too."
- Never explain or defend the product during the session. "Interesting, tell me more" is the answer to criticism.
